# Tuition, Fees, and Financial Obligations Policy

## Section 1: Introduction
The financial stability of the University relies heavily on the timely collection of tuition and associated fees. This policy outlines the financial obligations of all registered students, the structure of fees, acceptable payment methodologies, and the consequences of defaulting on financial commitments. By registering for courses, students enter into a binding financial contract with the University and agree to abide by the terms set forth in this policy. The University reserves the right to adjust fee structures annually to reflect inflationary pressures and enhanced service provisions.

## Section 2: Fee Structure and Categories
The total financial obligation of a student comprises several categories. The primary component is the Tuition Fee, which varies depending on the specific academic program, the student's residency status (domestic versus international), and the number of credits registered. Additionally, students are liable for a mandatory Student Services Fee, which funds campus life initiatives, health services, and recreational facilities. Other potential charges include laboratory fees for science and engineering courses, technology fees for specialized software access, and administrative fees for specific services such as transcript requests or late registrations.

## Section 3: Payment Methods and Schedules
The University offers multiple secure avenues for the remittance of fees. Payments can be made via direct bank transfer, certified cashier's cheque, or major credit cards through the secure online payment portal. Cash payments are not accepted at any university office for security reasons. The University also offers an optional installment payment plan for eligible domestic students, allowing tuition fees to be spread over three equal monthly payments. Detailed payment schedules and deadlines are published on the official University academic calendar well in advance of the semester commencement.

## Section 4: Late Payments and Penalties
Adherence to financial deadlines is strictly enforced to ensure smooth administrative operations. All tuition fees must be paid in full by the end of the second week of the semester. Failure to do so will result in immediate suspension of university services, including library access and course registration. This suspension encompasses both physical access to facilities and digital access to the online learning management system. Furthermore, accounts in arrears will be assessed a non-refundable late fee of 5% of the outstanding balance, compounded monthly until the debt is settled in full.

## Section 5: Refunds and Withdrawals
Students who choose to withdraw from their academic program or drop specific courses may be eligible for a partial refund of tuition fees, contingent upon the timing of the withdrawal. Withdrawals processed before the start of the semester incur no financial penalty. A sliding scale applies thereafter: a 75% refund if processed during the first week, a 50% refund during the second week, and no refunds will be issued for withdrawals processed after the second week of classes. Mandatory student services fees are strictly non-refundable once the semester has commenced.

## Section 6: International Student Fees
International students are subject to a differential tuition fee structure reflecting the unsubsidized cost of their education. In addition to the standard tuition and service fees, international students must demonstrate proof of comprehensive health insurance coverage valid within the country for the duration of their studies. The University offers an approved health insurance plan, the premium for which is automatically added to the student's account unless a valid waiver is approved prior to the start of the semester.

## Section 7: Miscellaneous Fees
Various administrative processes attract specific miscellaneous fees. These include, but are not limited to, fees for the issuance of replacement ID cards, processing of late graduation applications, supplementary examination registrations, and formal grade appeal requests. A comprehensive schedule of miscellaneous fees is maintained by the Bursary Office and is available on the university website. These fees are payable at the point of service request and are universally non-refundable.

## Section 8: Fee Waivers
In exceptional circumstances, the University may grant partial or full fee waivers. These are primarily allocated to staff dependents as part of the employee benefits package, or to high-performing graduate students serving as Teaching or Research Assistants. Applications for fee waivers must be processed and approved by Human Resources or the School of Graduate Studies, respectively, and submitted to the Bursary Office prior to the generation of the semester fee invoice.

## Section 9: Tax Receipts
The University automatically generates annual tax receipts (T2202A or equivalent) for eligible tuition fees paid during the preceding calendar year. These receipts are made available for secure download via the student portal by the end of February each year. It is the student's responsibility to ensure their Social Insurance Number or equivalent tax identification number is correctly recorded in the Student Information System to facilitate the accurate generation of these tax documents.

## Section 10: Policy Review
The Bursary Office, in consultation with the University Finance Committee, is responsible for the biennial review of this financial policy. The aim of this review is to ensure the policy remains equitable, transparent, and aligned with the strategic financial goals of the institution. Any significant alterations to the fee structure or payment regulations will be communicated to the student body no later than three months prior to the commencement of the academic year affected by the changes.
