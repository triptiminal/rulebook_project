# Student Financial Support, Scholarships, and Bursaries Framework

## Section 1: Overview
The University firmly believes that financial constraints should not be a barrier to accessing high-quality higher education. To this end, the institution has established a comprehensive Student Financial Support, Scholarships, and Bursaries Framework designed to recognize academic excellence, support students from economically disadvantaged backgrounds, and promote diversity within the student body. The administration of these funds is overseen by the Office of Financial Aid, ensuring that all awards are distributed fairly, transparently, and in accordance with the strategic priorities of the University and the specific wishes of external donors.

## Section 2: Types of Financial Aid Available
The financial support offered by the University falls into two broad categories: Scholarships and Bursaries. Scholarships are primarily merit-based awards, recognizing outstanding academic achievement, exceptional talent in athletics or the arts, or demonstrated leadership potential. Bursaries, on the other hand, are strictly need-based grants provided to students who can demonstrate significant financial hardship that might otherwise prevent them from commencing or continuing their studies. The University also facilitates access to government-backed student loan schemes and operates a limited campus work-study program.

## Section 3: Eligibility Criteria for Merit-Based Scholarships
To be considered for a merit-based University Scholarship, a student must typically be enrolled full-time in a degree-granting program. Entering undergraduate students are evaluated based on their high school academic record, standardized test scores, and any submitted portfolios or audition results. Continuing students are assessed based on their cumulative grade point average (CGPA) at the University, with most prestigious awards requiring a minimum CGPA of 3.8 out of 4.0. Specific scholarships may also have additional criteria, such as enrollment in a particular faculty or demonstrated commitment to community service.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria for Need-Based Bursaries
The assessment for need-based bursaries is highly rigorous and requires students to submit a detailed financial disclosure form. This form requires the declaration of all sources of income, including parental contributions, personal savings, part-time employment earnings, and any other external financial aid. Applicants must also provide supporting documentation, such as recent tax returns or bank statements. The Office of Financial Aid utilizes a standardized formula to calculate the 'Expected Family Contribution' and determines the level of unmet financial need, which dictates the value of the bursary awarded.

## Section 5: Application Procedures
The application cycle for most scholarships and bursaries aligns with the start of the academic year. The main application portal opens in early Spring, with a firm deadline typically set for late May. Students are required to complete a centralized online application that automatically matches their profile against the eligibility criteria of all available awards. Some specialized scholarships may require additional materials, such as an essay, a research proposal, or letters of recommendation from academic referees. Incomplete or late applications are not considered under any circumstances.

## Section 6: Selection Committees and Processes
The selection process for financial awards is designed to be impartial and rigorous. For major university-wide scholarships, applications are reviewed by the Central Scholarship Committee, composed of senior faculty members and representatives from the Office of Financial Aid. Departmental scholarships are adjudicated by specific faculty committees. These committees rank candidates based on the established criteria and make final recommendations to the Director of Financial Aid, who provides the final sign-off to ensure all procedural and budgetary requirements have been met.

## Section 7: Disbursement of Funds
Financial awards are not typically disbursed as cash payments directly to the student. Instead, the value of the scholarship or bursary is applied directly as a credit against the student's tuition and fees account with the Bursary Office. For awards that exceed the total amount of tuition and fees owed, the remaining balance may be issued to the student via direct deposit to assist with living expenses, such as housing or textbooks. Funds are usually disbursed in two equal installments at the beginning of the Fall and Spring semesters.

## Section 8: Maintenance of Awards
Many scholarships are renewable for the duration of a student's degree program, provided the student maintains the required academic standards. Students holding renewable merit-based scholarships are generally required to maintain a minimum CGPA, often 3.5 or higher, and complete a full course load each academic year. The Office of Financial Aid conducts an annual academic review at the end of the Spring semester to determine eligibility for renewal. Students who fail to meet the renewal criteria will be formally notified that their award has been discontinued.

## Section 9: Termination of Scholarships
In addition to failing to meet academic renewal criteria, a scholarship or bursary may be terminated prematurely for several reasons. These include: the student withdrawing from the University or dropping below full-time enrollment status; the student being found guilty of a severe academic or non-academic disciplinary offense; or the discovery that the student provided fraudulent or misleading information on their financial aid application. The University reserves the right to demand the repayment of any funds disbursed based on fraudulent claims.

## Section 10: Endowed and External Scholarships
The University administers a significant number of endowed scholarships established through the generosity of alumni, corporate partners, and philanthropic foundations. The specific criteria for these awards are governed by the Deed of Gift agreed upon with the donor. Furthermore, the Office of Financial Aid acts as a clearinghouse for information regarding external scholarships offered by government agencies and independent organizations. While the University does not select the recipients for these external awards, it provides administrative support in verifying enrollment and academic standing for the successful candidates.
