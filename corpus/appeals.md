# Student Grievance Redressal and Appeals Procedure

## Section 1: Purpose
The University is deeply committed to providing an educational environment that is fair, equitable, and conducive to academic and personal growth. However, it recognizes that disputes, misunderstandings, or grievances may arise during a student's tenure. This Student Grievance Redressal and Appeals Procedure provides a formal, transparent, and structured mechanism for students to seek resolution for complaints related to academic decisions, administrative actions, or interpersonal conflicts within the university community. The primary goal is to ensure swift and impartial resolution while upholding the principles of natural justice.

## Section 2: Definitions of Grievances
For the context of this procedure, a grievance is defined as a formal complaint lodged by a student concerning an action or decision made by a university staff member, faculty member, or administrative department that the student believes to be unfair, discriminatory, or contrary to published university policy. Grievances are broadly categorized into Academic Grievances (e.g., disputes over grading, academic standing, or allegations of academic misconduct) and Non-Academic Grievances (e.g., issues concerning university housing, financial administration, disciplinary actions, or harassment).

## Section 3: Informal Resolution Mechanisms
The University strongly advocates for the resolution of grievances at the lowest possible level through open dialogue and mediation. Before initiating a formal appeal, students are expected to make a concerted effort to resolve the issue informally. This typically involves discussing the concern directly with the individual involved, such as the course instructor or the specific administrative staff member. If direct communication is uncomfortable or unsuccessful, the student may seek the assistance of their Academic Advisor or a designated departmental mediator to facilitate a constructive conversation and seek a mutually agreeable solution.

## Section 4: Formal Appeal Procedures
If informal resolution fails, or if the grievance is of a severe nature, the student may initiate a formal appeal. The formal appeal must be submitted in writing to the relevant Faculty Dean or the Dean of Students within fifteen working days of the incident or the failed informal resolution attempt. The written appeal must clearly outline the nature of the grievance, the specific university policies alleged to have been violated, a chronological summary of events, and the desired outcome or remedy. All relevant supporting documentation, such as email correspondence or medical certificates, must be attached to the formal submission.

## Section 5: Academic Appeals
Academic appeals concerning final course grades or decisions related to academic standing are reviewed by the Faculty Academic Appeals Committee. The Committee operates under a strict mandate to determine if the original decision was reached fairly and in accordance with the established grading rubric or academic policy. The Committee will not simply re-grade an assessment based on a difference of academic opinion. A successful academic appeal typically requires demonstrating procedural error, demonstrable bias on the part of the examiner, or significant extenuating circumstances that were not appropriately considered during the initial assessment process.

## Section 6: Financial Appeals and Extensions
Appeals related to tuition, fees, or financial penalties are adjudicated by the Financial Appeals Board. The Board considers cases involving significant, unforeseen financial hardship or administrative errors made by the University in calculating financial obligations. Students facing financial hardship may apply for a fee extension up to the fourth week of the semester. During this grace period, all student privileges, including library and academic portal access, remain fully active. Applications for financial appeals must include comprehensive documentation demonstrating the financial distress, such as bank statements or letters from sponsors.

## Section 7: Role of the Ombudsman
The Office of the University Ombudsman serves as an independent, impartial, and confidential resource for students navigating the grievance process. The Ombudsman does not act as an advocate for either the student or the University but rather as a neutral facilitator ensuring procedural fairness. The Ombudsman can provide guidance on interpreting university policies, advise on the appropriate channels for lodging an appeal, and assist in mediating complex disputes before they escalate to a formal committee hearing.

## Section 8: Timelines and Deadlines
Adherence to established timelines is critical for the efficient operation of the appeals process. As stated, formal appeals must be lodged within fifteen working days of the instigating event. The relevant appeals committee is required to acknowledge receipt of the appeal within five working days. A formal hearing, if deemed necessary, will typically be scheduled within twenty working days of receipt. The committee aims to provide a final, written decision to the student within ten working days following the conclusion of the hearing or the final review of the submitted documentation.

## Section 9: Confidentiality and Non-Retaliation
The University guarantees that all grievance and appeal proceedings will be conducted with the highest degree of confidentiality, sharing information only on a need-to-know basis with those directly involved in the adjudication process. Furthermore, the University strictly prohibits any form of retaliation, discrimination, or adverse action against a student for initiating a grievance or participating in an appeal process in good faith. Any allegations of retaliation will be treated as a severe disciplinary offense and investigated independently.

## Section 10: Final Arbiters
The decisions reached by the highest-level designated appeals committees (such as the Senate Appeals Committee for academic matters or the Executive Financial Board for financial matters) are considered final and binding within the University's internal administrative structure. If a student remains dissatisfied with the outcome after exhausting all internal procedures, they may explore external avenues for redress, such as lodging a complaint with the national higher education ombudsman or seeking independent legal counsel, though the University is not obliged to participate in or abide by non-statutory external reviews.
