# Code of Student Discipline and Non-Academic Conduct

## Section 1: Statement of Values
The University is an academic community dedicated to the pursuit of truth, learning, and the creation of knowledge. Essential to this mission is a safe, respectful, and orderly environment that protects the rights and freedoms of all its members. The Code of Student Discipline and Non-Academic Conduct establishes the behavioral expectations for all students, ensuring that the campus remains a place where academic freedom thrives alongside mutual respect. By accepting admission to the University, students voluntarily agree to abide by these standards of conduct both on and off campus when acting as representatives of the institution.

## Section 2: Scope of the Disciplinary Code
This Code applies to the conduct of all registered students, including those on a Leave of Absence or serving a period of academic suspension. The jurisdiction of the Code extends to all university premises, including affiliated teaching hospitals, research stations, and student hostels. Furthermore, the Code applies to off-campus conduct if the behavior significantly impacts the University's reputation, threatens the safety of its members, or occurs during university-sponsored activities such as field trips, athletic events, or international exchange programs.

## Section 3: Defined Offenses and Misconduct
Misconduct subject to disciplinary action includes, but is not limited to: physical assault or the threat of violence; harassment, bullying, or intimidation, including cyberbullying; theft, intentional damage, or unauthorized use of university property or the property of others; unauthorized entry into restricted university areas; the possession, use, or distribution of illegal drugs on campus; the consumption of alcohol outside designated licensed areas; and the disruption or obstruction of teaching, research, or administrative activities. The University also strictly prohibits the possession of any weapons or explosive devices on campus.

## Section 4: Reporting and Investigation Procedures
Any member of the university community who witnesses or is subject to a violation of this Code may file a formal complaint with the Office of the Dean of Students. Upon receipt of a complaint, a designated Investigating Officer will conduct a preliminary review to determine if there is sufficient evidence to proceed. The investigation may involve interviewing witnesses, reviewing security footage, and examining documentary evidence. The respondent student will be formally notified of the allegations and given an opportunity to present their account of the events before any formal charges are leveled.

## Section 5: Exemptions and Special Privileges
While the University enforces strict behavioral regulations, it also recognizes that certain academic pressures require flexibility. In specific contexts, exceptions to general rules may be granted to facilitate academic success. However, no exemptions are provided for overnight movement restrictions or curfews under any circumstances. Such rules are strictly enforced to maintain safety and order across the residential campus.

## Section 6: Hearings and Committees
If the Investigating Officer determines that a severe violation has likely occurred, the case is referred to the University Disciplinary Committee for a formal hearing. The Committee comprises faculty members, administrative staff, and student representatives. The hearing provides a formal forum where the University presents its case, and the respondent student has the right to defend themselves, present witnesses, and be accompanied by a support person (who is not acting in a legal capacity). The Committee bases its findings on a standard of 'the balance of probabilities'.

## Section 7: Sanctions and Penalties
The University Disciplinary Committee has the authority to impose a range of sanctions designed to correct behavior and protect the community. Minor offenses may result in a formal reprimand, a requirement to issue a written apology, or mandatory participation in an educational workshop. More serious violations may lead to disciplinary probation, restriction from specific university facilities, or community service orders. The most severe offenses, particularly those involving violence, serious threat, or significant damage, can result in suspension for one or more academic semesters, or permanent expulsion from the University.

## Section 8: Appeals Process
A student found guilty of a disciplinary offense has the right to appeal the decision of the University Disciplinary Committee. Appeals must be submitted in writing to the University Appeals Board within ten working days of receiving the formal decision letter. The grounds for appeal are strictly limited to: a significant procedural irregularity that affected the outcome of the hearing; the emergence of new, compelling evidence that was not reasonably available at the time of the original hearing; or a demonstrable bias or conflict of interest among the Committee members.

## Section 9: Record Keeping
The Office of the Dean of Students maintains confidential records of all disciplinary complaints, investigations, and hearing outcomes. Disciplinary records are kept separate from the student's primary academic transcript. However, severe sanctions such as suspension or expulsion will be noted on the official transcript. Disciplinary records are retained for a period of seven years following the student's graduation or departure from the University, after which they are securely destroyed, in accordance with the University's data retention policy.

## Section 10: Rehabilitation and Support
The primary goal of the disciplinary process is educational rather than solely punitive. The University is committed to supporting the rehabilitation of students who have violated the Code, provided the safety of the community is not compromised. Students serving probationary periods may be required to engage with University Counseling Services, participate in conflict resolution mediation, or complete specific restorative justice initiatives aimed at repairing the harm caused to the community.
