# Comprehensive Examination Guidelines and Academic Assessment Policy

## Section 1: Preamble
The University is committed to fostering an environment of academic excellence, rigor, and integrity. The evaluation of student learning through examinations is a cornerstone of this commitment. This Comprehensive Examination Guidelines and Academic Assessment Policy is designed to ensure that all assessment practices are fair, transparent, consistent, and aligned with the intended learning outcomes of the University's diverse academic programs. These guidelines apply to all forms of summative assessment, including mid-term examinations, final examinations, practical assessments, and comprehensive oral defenses.

## Section 2: Eligibility for Examinations
To be eligible to sit for any formal university examination, students must fulfill several prerequisite conditions. Primarily, students must be officially registered for the course and have cleared all outstanding financial dues to the University. Furthermore, academic engagement is critical. A student possessing a valid medical certificate approved by the University Medical Officer may be permitted to write the final examination provided their attendance is not less than 60%. Such exemptions are intended to support students experiencing genuine and documented health crises that significantly impact their ability to attend scheduled sessions.

## Section 3: Registration and Scheduling
Examination schedules are formulated by the Office of the Registrar and published no later than six weeks prior to the commencement of the examination period. It is the sole responsibility of the student to verify their examination timetable and report any direct clashes (two examinations scheduled at the exact same time) within five working days of the publication date. Registration for examinations is automatic for courses in which the student is enrolled. However, supplementary or retake examinations require a separate, formal registration process and the payment of associated administrative fees.

## Section 4: Conduct During Examinations
The University mandates strict adherence to established protocols during all examinations to maintain a secure and equitable testing environment. Students must arrive at the examination hall at least fifteen minutes before the scheduled start time. Latecomers may be admitted up to thirty minutes after the commencement of the exam, but no extra time will be granted. Valid university identification must be prominently displayed on the desk at all times. The use of unauthorized electronic devices, including mobile phones and programmable calculators, is strictly prohibited and constitutes a severe breach of academic integrity.

## Section 5: Academic Integrity and Malpractice
Academic malpractice encompasses any action that seeks to gain an unfair advantage in an assessment. This includes, but is not limited to, plagiarism, collusion, impersonation, bringing unauthorized materials into the examination hall, and communicating with other candidates during the exam. All suspected instances of malpractice are investigated rigorously by the Academic Disciplinary Board. Proven offenses can result in penalties ranging from a zero grade for the specific assessment to suspension or permanent expulsion from the University, depending on the severity and frequency of the violation.

## Section 6: Grading Systems and Transcripts
The University employs a standardized grading system based on a cumulative grade point average (CGPA) scale. Final grades are determined by an aggregate of continuous assessments and the final examination, as specified in the course syllabus. Course instructors must submit final grades to the Registry within two weeks of the examination date. Official transcripts, reflecting the student's complete academic record, can be requested through the student portal. The University ensures the confidentiality of student academic records in compliance with relevant data protection legislation.

## Section 7: Special Arrangements for Students with Disabilities
The University is dedicated to providing equal opportunities for all students. Candidates with documented physical, sensory, or specific learning disabilities may apply for reasonable accommodations during examinations. Such arrangements may include extended writing time, the use of a scribe or reader, alternative assessment formats, or testing in a separate, distraction-reduced environment. Applications for special arrangements must be submitted to the Disability Support Office at the beginning of the academic semester, accompanied by current and appropriate medical or psychological documentation.

## Section 8: Post-Examination Review and Re-evaluation
Students who believe their examination paper has been incorrectly graded have the right to request a formal re-evaluation. This process involves a blind re-marking of the script by a second, independent examiner. The request for re-evaluation must be submitted within ten working days of the official publication of results, along with the prescribed administrative fee. If the re-evaluation results in a grade change, the administrative fee will be fully refunded. The grade awarded after re-evaluation, whether higher or lower, shall be final and binding.

## Section 9: Deferred Examinations
A deferred examination may be granted to a student who is unable to attend a scheduled final examination due to sudden, severe, and documented extenuating circumstances, such as acute illness or bereavement. Applications for deferred examinations must be submitted to the Academic Registry within 48 hours of the missed examination, supported by compelling evidence. Deferred examinations are typically scheduled during the designated supplementary examination period. Granting a deferred examination is at the discretion of the Dean of the relevant faculty.

## Section 10: Graduation Requirements
To qualify for graduation and the conferral of a degree, a student must successfully complete all compulsory modules, achieve the minimum required total credit hours for their specific program, and maintain a CGPA above the designated threshold. Additionally, all financial obligations to the University must be settled, and all university property, such as library books and laboratory equipment, must be returned. The final list of graduands must be ratified by the University Senate prior to the graduation ceremony.
